Six sequencers, six ways to write music

Each one takes a different archetype from a survey of sequencers (hardware, modular, software, live code) and builds it for the browser: its own sound, Web MIDI out, saving, undo and share links. Press Space to play. Their siblings for the M-VAVE FM-1 are LEDGER-4, MYCEL-8, SPOOL-4, REGENT-4, TERRACE-8 and CANON-4.

Trig-and-lock step groovebox

STITCH

Eight tracks of steps with their own lengths, conditions, ratchets, microtiming and parameter locks; patterns, fills and a snap-back.

After Elektron and the x0x machines Clip and scene launcher

QUILT

A grid of clips by track and scene, launched on the bar, with follow actions so a set can play itself, and a clip editor.

After Ableton Live's Session view and Bitwig Pattern language / live code

STRAND

Type rhythms and melodies in a mini-notation; each line is a voice, changed on the next cycle as you edit.

After TidalCycles and Strudel Cellular automaton

LICHEN

A field of cells that lives by the rules of Life (and others); the playhead sweeps it and every live cell sings.

After Game of Life sequencers, Zellen and Otomata Circular polyrhythm

ORBIT

Concentric rings of steps, each with its own count and cycle, turning against each other until they meet again.

After Patterning and rhythm necklaces Topographic drum map

TOPO

Drag across a map of grooves; patterns morph between regions, with density per drum, chaos and recorded motion.

After Mutable Instruments Grids (original map)
